PROJECT AETHYRA

Digital Twin & Multi-Scale Hydrology Simulation Report

DATE: August 2026 | SUBJECT: Solapur City Case Study & Cosmic Scaling

1. Executive Summary

Project Aethyra is an advanced Digital Twin ecosystem designed to map, simulate, and predict the flow, consumption, and scarcity of water resources. This case study focuses on Solapur, a historically drought-prone district in Maharashtra, India. By ingesting real-time data from 2026, the Aethyra module simulates water life-cycles from reservoir inputs to multi-sector outputs, featuring an "Accelerated Time" engine to predict future crises. The report then scales this concept from a hyper-local city grid to a universal scale.

2. Solapur 2026: Baseline Data & Ecosystem

A. Demographics & Population

  • Solapur City Population (2026 Est.): ~1.2 Million
  • Solapur District Population (2026 Est.): ~4.8 Million
  • Animal & Livestock Population: ~1.8 Million (Cattle, Buffaloes, Sheep, Goats - highly relevant for rural water consumption).

B. Industrial Footprint

  • Industrial Zones: 9 major MIDC (Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation) areas (e.g., Chincholi, Akkalkot Road).
  • Scale & Type: Solapur is a massive textile hub (Terry Towels, Chaddars), alongside Beedi manufacturing, and most importantly, Sugar Factories (over 30 operational sugar mills in the district).
  • Industrial Water Demand: Very High (Textile dyeing and sugarcane processing are heavily water-intensive).

C. Agriculture & Ecology

  • Agricultural Area: ~1.5 Million Hectares (approx. 15,000 sq. km).
  • Major Crops: Sugarcane (Cash crop, highest water consumer), Jowar, Pomegranate, Tur (Pigeon Pea).
  • Forest Cover: Extremely low, covering roughly 2.5% to 3% of the total geographical area, resulting in minimal natural water retention.

D. Climate & Rainfall (2026 Data)

2026 Status: Solapur typically receives an annual average of 600mm to 750mm. As of August 2026, cumulative rainfall recorded is hovering around 121.3 mm to 250 mm (varying by taluka), showing intermittent dry spells typical of its bimodal rainfall pattern.

3. Water Sources & Capacity Matrix

The lifeblood of Solapur is the Bhima River basin and its primary reservoir.

  • Primary Source: Ujani Dam (Bhima River)
  • Total Capacity: ~117.22 TMC (Thousand Million Cubic feet)
  • Live Storage Capacity: ~53.5 TMC
  • 2026 Real-Time Status: After dipping into minus/dead storage (-15 TMC) earlier in the year, heavy monsoon catchments in the Pune/Western Ghats region filled the Ujani Dam to 99.78% capacity by mid-August 2026.
  • Secondary Sources: Hipparga Lake (Ekrukh), Sina River, and local groundwater tables (which are largely depleted).

4. Requirement & Consumption Simulation (The Math)

To fuel the Aethyra simulation, we input the daily water withdrawal rates.

  1. Domestic Requirement (City):
    Standard requirement: 135 LPCD. 1.2 Million people × 135 L = 162 MLD.
  2. Industrial Requirement:
    Estimated at 45 MLD (Textile, MIDC). Note: Our simulation model offsets this with recycled sugarcane juice water.
  3. Agricultural Requirement:
    Sugarcane alone consumes nearly 70% of the district's irrigated water. In simulation, agriculture accounts for thousands of MLDs (~4200 MLD), creating the primary stress on the Ujani Dam.
  4. Forest & Animal Requirement:
    Livestock requires approx. 60-80 MLD across the district.

5. The Scaling Scenario: Solapur to the Cosmos

Project Aethyra is designed to zoom out continuously, demonstrating the interconnectedness of water.

Level 1: Micro - Solapur City

Focus: Pipeline leaks, Ujani levels, sugarcane vs. human survival. Simulation: Fluid dynamics of city pipes and local canals.

Level 2: Meso - Maharashtra State

Focus: Western Ghats catching Arabian Sea monsoon clouds. Upstream dams controlling downstream fate.

Level 3 & 4: Macro & Global

Focus: Monsoon weather systems, Himalayan glaciers, global ocean currents, and closed-loop water cycles.

Level 5: Cosmic

Focus: Interstellar Molecular Clouds. The water in Solapur's pipelines is alien, older than the sun, forged in supernovae.
